About

I am an Associate Staff Scientist at SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory and Stanford University. My research focuses on understanding plastic deconstruction and recycling through chemical and biological pathways, as well as developing autonomous and data-driven X-ray scattering methodologies for studying polymers and soft materials. By combining in situ and operando X-ray scattering with automated analysis and adaptive measurement strategies, my work aims to accelerate the discovery of structure–reactivity and structure–property relationships.

I earned my Ph.D. in Biomedical Engineering from the Swiss Federal Laboratories for Materials Science and Technology (Empa) and the University of Bern, where my research applied materials science approaches to biomedical systems. I completed the Erasmus Mundus Master's program in Materials Science Exploring Large Scale Facilities (MaMaSELF), studying at the University of Rennes 1 in France and the Technical University of Munich in Germany, and hold a B.Tech. in Engineering Physics from the Indian Institute of Technology Guwahati, India.

Driven by curiosity and impact, my work advances sustainable materials and chemical technologies while developing autonomous X-ray experimentation to accelerate discovery in energy-relevant and environmentally responsible systems.

X-ray Scattering Resources

This section shares practical resources based on my experience with SAXS and WAXS. I aim to highlight useful tools, common challenges, and open questions in the field. Feel free to reach out if you would like to discuss data, methods, or analysis—I am always happy to connect when time allows.

Recommended Readings

  • Elementary Scattering Theory: For X-ray and Neutron Users by Dr. D. S. Sivia – A fundamental resource for understanding scattering techniques.

Useful Links

  • SAS Tutorials – Educational material for SAXS/SANS, suitable for both beginners and advanced users.
  • Diamond Light Source SAXS Software List – A curated overview of commonly used SAXS software and processing tools.
  • X-ray Absorption Database – A comprehensive resource for X-ray absorption data.
  • Periodic Table Python Package – A Python package for easy access to periodic table data.

Community & Standards

  • canSAS (Collective Action for Nominal Small-Angle Scattering) – A community-driven initiative focused on SAXS standards and interoperability.
  • lightsources.org – A historical archive and directory of synchrotron and FEL facilities worldwide.
  • smallangle.org – A central hub for the global small-angle scattering community.

Specialized Tools & Software

  • Shape2SAS (part of SOMO) – A modeling tool to simulate SAXS profiles based on particle shape.
  • pyFAI – A Python library for azimuthal integration, designed for SAXS/WAXS data processing.
  • FabIO – A Python library to read and write various formats of X-ray scattering images.
  • ATSAS – A comprehensive software suite for (bio) small-angle scattering data analysis.
  • BioXTAS RAW – Software tailored for bioSAXS.
  • DENSS – A tool for calculating 3D particle electron densities from 1D SAXS data.

This list of software and tools for SAXS is far from complete—a flaw that keeps things exciting and chaotic, particularly for beginners.
